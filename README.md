**Project Title: Degrees of protection: how well do different kinds of marine protected areas guard against non-native species?** 

**Abstract**
Marine protected areas (MPAs) have emerged as the primary place-based tool for ocean conservation. While MPAs are proven to have some ecological benefits, there is growing evidence that the ability to achieve these outcomes corresponds to an area’s level of protection. Current global conservation targets aim to significantly increase the number of MPAs in our oceans, which warrants a comprehensive understanding of the performance of MPAs in the face of numerous stressors. Non-native species (NNS) are recognized as one of the primary threats to marine biodiversity. Despite this, the potential for MPAs to mitigate the establishment and impacts of NNS remains understudied and existing studies offer highly variable results. This study aims to investigate this relationship within a network of MPAs along the Central California coast, with particular attention given to how the occurrence of NNS varies across three different levels of protection using standardized surveys in the rocky intertidal. Results from this study reveal that some NNS are rather widespread in the study area but generally remain at low densities. When compared to the results of surveys conducted five and ten years ago, there have been range expansions of some NNS in the study area as well as novel introductions of others. Additionally, results indicate that regardless of protection level, the proximity of sites to a source for NNS (i.e., harbor, bay) is a major driver of presence and abundance. Results from this study show that, at least within this region, designation as an MPA does not in itself provide significant resilience to invasion by NNS.

**Code Installation** 
Code, data, and outputs associated with this project is housed in an .Rproj. To access necessary files, download the entire repository as a zip file and open the .Rproj in RStudio. From here, separate R Scripts are available for models, community analyses, and frequency of occurrence calculations.
